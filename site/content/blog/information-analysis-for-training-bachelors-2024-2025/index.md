## About this edition

I introduced data analysis in sport through three questions: what does a measurement tell us, how does a computer represent it, and how can we compare it with other records? Students used Excel to organise the data and make those comparisons.

## Programme

### Module 1: Data science in sport

- **Sport measurements.** Heart rate readings, GPS positions and athletes’ own assessments illustrate biometric, movement and subjective data. Variables can be qualitative or quantitative, discrete or continuous. Nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio scales determine which comparisons make sense.
- **Populations and samples.** A census covers the whole population. Random, stratified and cluster sampling illustrate different ways to select athletes and how that choice affects the conclusions.

### Module 2: Representation and management of multimedia data

- **File storage.** Files, folders, paths and metadata describe how records are organised and located.
- **Signs and text.** Semiotics distinguishes a sign from its meaning. Character sets and encodings specify how a computer stores written characters.
- **Images.** Raster graphics represent an image with pixels; vector graphics describe shapes.
- **Sound and video.** Sampled audio records a signal at regular intervals, while MIDI messages describe musical events. Video represents movement through a sequence of frames.
- **Communication and compression.** The Shannon and Weaver model describes how a message reaches a receiver through a channel. Entropy and redundancy help explain compression. Lossless methods preserve the original data; lossy methods discard detail.

### Module 3: Data analysis with Microsoft Excel

- **Worksheets and formulas.** A gym budget illustrates how to enter and format values, work with cell ranges and calculate expenses. Topics include cell references, operators, function arguments and common formula errors.
- **Data import.** Power Query reads activity records from text files. Dates and decimal separators need the correct settings for Excel to recognise the values.
- **Charts.** Line and area charts show changes over time in Fitbit activity records. Scatter plots show how two measurements vary together.
- **PivotTables and PivotCharts.** Grouping Fitbit records by week makes it possible to compare steps, calories and minutes of intense activity. A PivotChart displays the summary.
