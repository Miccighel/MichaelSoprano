## About the course

Why does a message reach some parts of a social network but not others? When several people assess the same claim, how much do they agree? Social Computing explores these questions through network analysis and crowdsourcing. The course is taught in Italian at the University of Udine.

The first two editions listed here were part of the Bachelor's Degree in Internet of Things, Big Data & Web.

## Teaching

[Stefano Mizzaro](https://users.dimi.uniud.it/~stefano.mizzaro/) teaches the course. As a teaching assistant, I have taught practical sessions and prepared exercises and group assignments. My contribution has varied by year: in the early editions I taught both network analysis and crowdsourcing; in later editions I focused on crowdsourcing.

## Examples of practical work

- **Mapping a social network.** In [2020/2021](/post/social-computing/2020-2021/), students used Python to collect follower relationships on Twitter. They compared accounts with many connections to accounts that linked otherwise distant parts of the network.
- **Designing a rating task.** In [2023/2024](/post/social-computing/2023-2024/), students used Crowd_Frame to ask classmates to assess Amazon product reviews. The task asked whether a review was clear, reliable and informative, not whether the product was good.
- **Assessing political claims.** In [2025/2026](/post/social-computing/2025-2026/), students designed a task around three statements checked by PolitiFact. For each one, participants were asked how truthful they thought it was and how confident they were in that judgement, on a scale of 1 to 100.
