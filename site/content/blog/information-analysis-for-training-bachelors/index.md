## About the course

A finishing position tells us who came first, but only the race times tell us how far apart the runners finished. This course uses examples from sport to show what different measurements can tell us, how they are stored and how to compare them in Excel.

The course is taught in Italian under the title *Sistemi di Analisi ed Elaborazione delle Informazioni per l’Allenamento*. It is part of the integrated course *Bioengineering and Biomechanics of Physical and Sport Activities*.

## Teaching

I teach the course using examples from activity trackers and competition results. Before asking students to make a chart or calculate an average, I ask them to check what each row represents, what units are used and which records make sense to compare. Teaching hours and the choice of exercises vary by edition.

## Programme

- **Data science in sport.** Identify the observations and variables in training records, choose comparisons that suit the measurement scale and recognise what a sample can tell us about a larger group.
- **Data representation and management.** See how computers represent text, images, sound and video. Use files, metadata, units and timestamps to understand and organise records from different sources.
- **Data analysis with Microsoft Excel.** Import records with Power Query, check column types, and use formulas, summary statistics and charts to compare results.

## Examples of practical work

- **Comparing marathon finish times.** In [2025/2026](/post/information-analysis-for-training-bachelors/2025-2026/#programme), students used PivotTables to group Boston Marathon results by age group or gender and compare finish times.
- **Comparing lifting results.** In [2026/2027](/post/information-analysis-for-training-bachelors/2026-2027/#programme), students used OpenPowerlifting data to compare athletes by total weight lifted and by weight lifted relative to body weight.

## Assessment

The course is assessed by a written exam. Each question has one correct answer, worth one point. The number of questions varies by edition; a score of 31 or more earns 30/30 with honours.
